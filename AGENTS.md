# AGENTS.md

How Batteries is put together, for anyone (or any agent) changing it. The user-facing story is in [README.md](README.md).

## Layout

- `BarWidget.qml`: the bar entry, once per monitor. The first instance (the leader) loads `BatteryClient.qml`; the others read the leader's. Hosts `Panel.qml` and the IPC target.
- `BatteryClient.qml`: polls `bin/batteries probe`, listens to udev and librepods, keeps last-seen levels, the laptop's charge history and low-battery notifications. AirPods keep a last level per part (`Model.airpodsWithLastKnown`): a closed case takes itself and the bud inside off the air while the other bud is worn. Those levels are saved to `~/.local/state/omarchy-batteries/airpods.json`, but only when a level or what answers changes. The buds hear the case only while one of them sits in it, so with every bud in an ear the case is last seen (`caseOutOfReach`): what they still report for it, a charger included, is from when they left it.
- `Panel.qml`: the panel (see "Panel look" below). A prompt line beside the hero battery, then one `Room` per battery: icon, name over state, the level in `BigFigure`'s block digits, and a `Battery`. The laptop adds its readings, AirPods their left, right and case cells. A click opens a room (`root.openRoom`, one at a time) to a `Drawer` with its other readings (`Model.deviceFacts`, `airpodsFacts`, the rest of `laptopFacts`) and its bar switches (`Model.roomPicks`). Everything that moves runs off one clock (`root.clock`) that ticks only while the panel is open and something charges or a ripple runs.
- `Battery.qml`: a level as a battery of cells, a tenth each, filled to the full height. The cell maths (fill, charge pulse, breathing, the pointer's dent, ripples) is `Model.batteryCells`.
- `BigFigure.qml`: a level in the block face from `Model.bigDigits`, laid out on the font's character grid but drawn as rectangles, since typed block characters leave hairline seams.
- The panel kit (below) and the shared updater (`Updater.qml`, `Update.js`, `bin/plugin-update`).
- `WidgetSettings.qml`: this widget's `shell.json` entry, read from the file (see below).
- `Model.js`: parsing, the bar items, the history and alert rules. Tested by `tests/model.test.js`.
- `bin/batteries`: the reader (Python, stdlib only): sysfs, UPower, librepods, HID for Glorious and HyperX, JBL's GATT service, openrazer, HeadsetControl, libimobiledevice, KDE Connect. Tested by `tests/test_batteries.py`.
- `PixelIcon.qml`, `Pixels.js`: every icon in the panel is pixel art, one set: Pixelarticons (MIT), plus AirPods and their case drawn in `scripts/fetch-pixel-icons.py` on the same grid. The script turns each SVG into an exact grid of cells (24, and 12 for small sizes) and `PixelIcon` draws them on whole device pixels, picking the grid by the screen's scale so nothing blurs. Rooms name them directly (`Model.kindPixel`, `Model.markIcon`). Add an icon to the script's list and rerun it (it reads the SVGs in `pixels/`; `--fetch` downloads them again). The laptop is drawn there too: Pixelarticons' is two rows short of the square its neighbours fill and sat low in the bar.
- `Motion.js`: how each icon acts out what it is, the one place motion for icons lives. The script splits an icon into a still base and named `parts` (its `PARTS` boxes on the 12 grid), plus `extra` parts that show only mid-act (`EXTRAS`: the laptop's `>_` prompt, the clock's hand positions, the gear's pressed hub). An act is a flipbook of frames that move parts in whole 12-grid cells, hide them, show extras, or turn the whole icon in quarters; it ends at rest. `PixelIcon` draws each part on its own and plays the act: `play` going up plays it once after `delay`, `loop` repeats it on the wall clock while the icon can be seen (`Motion.LOOPS`, each period dividing `LOOP_MS`). Only the part that is the thing moves: the mouse's wheel, a key, the bell's skirt and clapper, a bud, the case's lid. Pixel art never turns or scales smoothly, and a 45 degree frame of a two-unit stroke never comes out clean, so nothing turns but by quarters. `tests/motion.test.js` checks every act against the art. `bin/batteries badge` draws an icon at rest.
- `Glyph.qml`, `Model.GLYPHS`, `Model.kindGlyph`: the icon the panel kit is handed, as Nerd Font glyphs (escapes, because the characters are invisible in most editors). `Glyph.qml` draws the pixel icon `Model.PIXEL_GLYPHS` gives a glyph, so the kit's icons match the rooms'. Map a new glyph there. The one typed glyph left is the "Check now" button in the shared `UpdateSection`.
- `BarIcon.qml` is a bar item's pixel icon, with a hard-edged dark band stepping up through it a cell at a time while charge flows in. On a charger but full or held at a limit, and with reduced motion, the band holds still over the middle (`Model.CHARGE_HOLD_FLOW`), so a connected charger still shows. Every step repaints the whole bar on every monitor, so `Model.chargeStep` takes exactly one step per cell of the band's travel (`CHARGE_STEPS`): more steps than cells stuttered, some moving the band and some not.
- `assets/fonts/`: Monocraft (OFL-1.1, its licence in `OFL.txt`), the panel's and hover card's text face, rebuilt by `scripts/patch-font.py` to ten font pixels to the em, an 11-pixel line (9 above, 2 below, no gap), `❯` and `▌`, and the family name "Monocraft Batteries": under one name, the other plugins' copies would answer for it. `BarWidget.qml` loads the regular face with a `FontLoader` and hands the family out as `textFont`; `Panel.fontFamily` takes it, so the panel kit gets it too. There is no bold: Monocraft's is a fifth of a pixel off the grid. The block digits (`BigFigure`) and typed Nerd Font glyphs (`Glyph`) keep the shell's font, since the digits are laid out on its character grid and only it has the glyphs.
- `MonoText.qml`: the kit's text, the same file in all four plugins. Every font pixel lands on whole device pixels: `step` picks `"small"`, `"body"` or `"title"`, a whole number of device pixels per font pixel for the window's scale (1/2/3 at 1 and 1.25, 2/3/4 at 1.5 and 2), and `fontFamily` takes the host's. It sets the font's family, size and weight, the render type, antialiasing and a transform that snaps it to the pixel grid; never set those on a MonoText. Read `line`, `advance` and `pixel` to line other things up with it.
- `Words.qml`: every line of the panel's and hover card's own text: a MonoText that wraps (`Text.Wrap`: between words, inside one only when it alone is too wide). Readings go through `Model.keepTogether`, so a line never breaks inside "2h 00m", "18 W" or "just now", or before a "·". The prompt is `›` and `_`, and the line is laid out whole from the first character and revealed as it types (`Model.revealLine`, Sonus's), so a word never jumps to the next line. Readings fall back to one a line when a value would wrap in half the width, and the hover card grows to fit its widest row on one line.
- `PixelText.qml`: a line in the pixel type (`Model.PIXEL_FONT`, `Model.pixelText`): the panel's block digits a column wider, the %, and the letters the bar writes. The bar's figures and its icons share whole device pixels; nothing is ever scaled, which smears pixel art. On a 1.25 scale bar icons are 12 device pixels, the largest sharp size under the shell's own.
- `HoverRow.qml`: one battery in the hover card, its own file so the offscreen harness can draw it (a popup's content cannot be grabbed).
- `HoverCard.qml`: the card the bar item opens on hover in place of the shell's plain tooltip (kept only for the access message), placed and framed like that tooltip. A row per battery (`Model.hoverRows`) with the mark in a column of its own, there on every row, so every row's battery and figure stay in one column. It grows from 280 to 380 to keep each row on one line, its icons act a row after the other (70 ms), and a dim last line says what middle and right click do (`Model.HOVER_HINT`).
- Low-battery notifications (`BatteryClient.sendAlert`, `Model.alertNote`, `Model.alertState`) are real notifications, so they stack with the shell's others, land in its history and keep to Do Not Disturb. A plugin cannot draw inside the shell's toast or learn where its stack is, so an overlay of our own would cover it; the look goes in through what a sender may set. The picture is a pixel badge in the panel's look, drawn by `bin/batteries badge` for the focused screen's scale (the shell's 40 slot, a whole number of device pixels a cell, so it is sharp) into `~/.local/state/omarchy-batteries/alerts/`. The `omarchy-exec-argv` hint makes a click open the panel. `notify-send -p --wait` stays running while the notification is up: plugged in, the same notification is replaced (`-r`) with its thanks in the room's colour and goes after five seconds; back above the line or out of reach, it is closed over D-Bus. `omarchy-shell io.github.t3lluz.batteries previewAlert` runs that life on a made-up 12%.
- `udev/70-omarchy-batteries.rules`: HID access for the logged-in user.

## Panel kit

`SectionHeader`, `StatTile`, `ToggleTile`, `SwitchRow`, `Seg`, `Notice`, `LevelBar`, `OptionRow`, `Caption` and `UpdateSection` in the plugin folder are the panel kit: the same files, byte for byte, in ROG Deck, Batteries, Sonus and Server Dashboard, so the four look like one family. Change a kit file in all four or not at all. `Glyph.qml` is the one part each plugin owns. The kit hands it Nerd Font glyphs from `Model.GLYPHS`; here it draws their pixel icons instead (`Model.PIXEL_GLYPHS`), so the kit matches the rooms without changing a kit file.

`Updater.qml`, `Update.js` and `bin/plugin-update` are the shared updater (ROG Deck, Batteries and Server Dashboard; Sonus's engine updates itself the same way). It stashes local edits, untracked files too, before fast-forwarding, and drops the stash when the edits already are the update. `tests/update.test.js` and `tests/test_plugin_update.py` cover it.

Panels never cut text: a `Notice` shows all of its message, a `Caption` breaks long words and paths, and a `ToggleTile` label that does not fit shows in full in its tooltip.

## Panel look

The battery panel has its own look, after the owner's Android apps (DailyDash and Weave) set in Omarchy's terminal type: rooms in a tone each, joined by small gaps, numbers in a block face, and motion that answers what happens. The settings page keeps the panel kit, so it matches the other plugins. Scaled through `Style`.

- Panel `Style.space(380)`, column gap 14. Rooms: padding 10, gap 3 between them, `Style.cornerRadius` (square on Omarchy's default), the tone at 0.065 alpha, 0.11 under the pointer.
- Tones: `Model.kindTone` picks a colour name per kind (power blue, audio magenta, input cyan, play green, phones yellow); `Model.roomToneHex` takes the theme's colour of that name from its `colors.toml` (read on each open) when it is a colour, else DailyDash's hue for it, and softens either a third of the way toward the text colour. A tone too faint on the background falls back to the foreground. Low uses `lowTone`. Only the panel and the hover card use tones; the bar stays in the bar's colours.
- Room header: pixel icon at `font.heading` × 1.25 (20) in the tone, the name in MonoText `body` with a `font.caption` chevron beside its first line that flips when the room is open, the state in `small`, dimmed, the level as `BigFigure` at `font.bodySmall` on the right (`font.caption` in an AirPods cell).
- Batteries: ten cells (five in an AirPods cell, four in the hero mark), track at 0.12 alpha, a 1 px outline at 0.6. Only the small hero mark has a nub; across a room it read as a stray pixel.
- Readings: `body`, the label dimmed, 4 between lines.
- Prompt: `›` and the caption from `Model.heroPhrases`, typed at 22 ms a character behind an `_` cursor blinking at 530 ms, a new line every 2800 ms. The whole line is laid out from the start and revealed (`Model.revealLine`). What needs attention holds still. What a control does goes in its tooltip.

Motion, all off with reduced motion:

- Opening: rooms rise 12 and fade in 70 ms apart; each battery fills from empty over 680 ms while its number counts up; states and readings decode out of scrambled glyphs (`Model.scramble`); then each icon acts out what it is once (`Motion.js`), a room after the other.
- Charging: a pulse runs up the filled cells every 1600 ms and the cell being filled breathes over 1100 ms; the bolt strikes top to bottom every 2400 ms. A low mark blinks on the same clock, and so does a warning in a notice. A busy notice's icon is the refresh arrows, turning in quarters every 200 ms (`reload-busy`).
- Icons act once when the panel opens, when the pointer comes into a room, on a click, when what a room stands for changes (charge starts or stops, it goes low or back, the device comes back), and in the settings when the page comes in (top to bottom) or a tile or row is switched (`Glyph` listens for the kit's `checked`). Otherwise they rest.
- Pointer: cells under it give (`press`); a click ripples out from it (Weave's 760 ms), plays the icon again and opens or closes the room, its drawer sliding over 280 ms. A click in an open drawer stays there (`Drawer` takes it), so only the rest of the room closes it.
- Keyboard: one cursor for keys and pointer, on a room by its key (`cursorKey`). `j` `k` or the arrows move it, Enter opens the room under it, a 1 px outline in the room's tone marks it, and only keyboard moves scroll it into view. `,` (and `c`) opens the settings, `r` reads now, Esc backs out a step: an open room, then the settings, then the panel (`back()`). The gear's tooltip names `(,)`, the arrow back `(esc)`. Right-clicking the bar item opens the settings.
- Changes while open: a level slides to its new value. Text decodes only on opening; a reading that changes every few seconds just changes, so it stays readable. The refresh arrows turn round in quarters when you read by hand; switching to the settings slides the page in from its side and presses the gear's hub.

Low must not depend on colour: a theme's red can be a dark grey (Solitude's is), so a low battery gets a mark, and `lowTone` falls back to the foreground when the theme's urgent colour has too little contrast.

## Rules that are easy to break

- **Settings** are read with `setting(key, fallback)` and saved with `persistSettings({...})`. Both go through `WidgetSettings.qml`, which reads `~/.config/omarchy/shell.json` itself. Never read `settings.foo` directly: the bar's copy turns a list into a Qt sequence that fails `Array.isArray`, and after a plugin reload it can be older than the file. Either one makes a pick look unset, which is how choices "reset after a reboot" or differ between monitors.
- **One widget per monitor.** The bar draws the widget on every screen. Long-running work (a poller, a client, a process) lives in one place, a leader instance or the service, and the others read from it. Never start it per instance.
- **IPC** is answered by whichever monitor's widget registered first, so `open`, `close` and `toggle` go through the shell's `summon`, `hide` and `toggle`, which pick the focused monitor.
- **Nothing may write inside the plugin folder at runtime.** The shell watches it and reloads every plugin when a file appears there. Python sets `sys.dont_write_bytecode`; state goes to `~/.local/state` or `~/.config`.
- **QML changes need `omarchy restart shell`.** Hot reload can keep the old component cached.
- **Repeat over a count, not a list rebuilt each poll.** A `Repeater` handed a new array destroys and rebuilds every delegate: a charging mouse replayed its room's whole opening at every percent, and switches lost their hover. Repeat over `list.length` and read `list[index]` (the device rooms, the bar items, the picks, the readings).
- **The scrollbar** is a 3 px bar laid over the rooms' right edge, shown only while `panel.overflowing`, worked out from what the panel wants against its height limit, never from the Flickable's height (that flashed while a room opened). `contentY` is clamped when the content shrinks.
- **Dim** is the text tinted toward the background (`Qt.tint(fg, rgba(bg, 0.4))`), never `Qt.darker`, which vanishes on a light theme.
- **Text is MonoText (or `Words`, which wraps one), never a `Text` with a hand-set size.** Hierarchy is the step and the colour, never weight: titles `title`; names, readings and rows `body` in the text colour; states, hints, captions, the prompt and section headers `small`, dimmed. At 1.25 body is twice small, so check wrapping and room heights there. Read the scale from the window (`Window.window.devicePixelRatio`), never `Screen.devicePixelRatio`, which says 2 on a 1.25 monitor; `PixelIcon`, `PixelText`, `MonoText` and the bar widget all do.
- **Check layout offscreen**, not on the live shell: run `Panel.qml` in a second quickshell with `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`, the shell's own `Commons` and a copy of its `Ui` where `Panel.qml` and `KeyboardPanel.qml` are plain-Item stand-ins (no popup, so the panel can be grabbed), a stand-in host, client and settings, and the worst data: long names, every state, a room open, the settings. Render at `QT_SCALE_FACTOR=2` and `1.25`, and with a light theme through a throwaway `HOME` whose `.local/state/omarchy/current/theme` points at it. The Rooms brief (`files get key:roomstheme`, "Auditing offscreen") has the rest.
- **Pure logic goes in `Model.js`** (a `.pragma library` with no QML) so `node tests/*.test.js` can run it. Change it with a test.
- Comments say why, in plain sentences. Match the surrounding code.

## Release

Bump `version` in `manifest.json` and the version badge in `README.md` together, run the tests, and describe the change for the person using it in the commit message.
